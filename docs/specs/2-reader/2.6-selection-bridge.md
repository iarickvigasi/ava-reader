## Summary
Turns a raw DOM text selection into a structured, anchored intent that the highlight and AI overlays
can act on.

## Scope
- In: capturing the active selection, holding it in context, deriving a range locator from it,
  exposing it to highlight and AI-toolbox overlays.
- Non-goals: persisting highlights (see 5.1-highlights), generating AI output (see 5.2-ai-toolbox /
  5.3-ai-comments), locator internals (see 2.4-locators).

## Behaviour
1. use-reader-text-selection observes the browser Selection and normalizes it to a reader range
   (chapter, blocks, offsets, text). On iOS it instead runs the app's own selection (Behaviour 8) —
   the native one is unusable there.
2. Capture fires on the tick after the gesture's end event (touchend / mouseup) and is read-only:
   it reports text + range + `pointer` kind and never mutates the selection. Opening the panel is
   what removes a touch capture's live selection — never anything before it opens.
3. reader-selection-context publishes the current selection so overlays render relative to it.
4. compute-ai-comment-locator derives a ReaderRangeLocator (with contextBefore/After) from the
   selection for AI tools.
5. use-highlight-selection-bridge converts the selection into a highlight intent (excerpt + locator)
   for the highlights bucket.
6. Selection clears on dismiss, navigation, or empty re-select.
7. On touch, selecting and page-swiping are mutually exclusive: while a non-empty selection is
   active inside the reader, the swipe handler suppresses page navigation
   (resolveSwipeNavigationOutcome), so extending or adjusting a selection never turns the page. This
   is touch-only — desktop selection uses mouse events, which have no swipe path.

8. **iOS only — the app owns selection.** iOS draws the selection highlight itself and refuses to
   draw it for a paragraph continued from an earlier column, which is most pages of a paginated
   book, so a native selection is routinely invisible while the reader is being used. On iOS the
   article is therefore `user-select: none` and the reader implements selection itself: long-press
   picks the word under the finger, dragging extends the range, the endpoints can be grabbed and
   moved, and the range is painted by the app as positioned rects. Release captures exactly as the
   native path does, so the panel and locator pipeline are unchanged. Android and desktop keep the
   native selection, which works there.
9. **iOS selection units.** Resolve a book-wide mode from `book.language` when opening the book,
   never from the UI locale or the touched text. Language tags using Latin, Cyrillic, or Greek
   script select words; other scripts (including Japanese, Chinese, and Arabic), unknown/invalid
   language, and multilingual books select graphemes (visible characters). Honor explicit script
   subtags and normalize known legacy language names. Foreign-language quotations follow the book's
   mode. Whitespace, zero-width spaces, and dash punctuation (including `-`, `—`, and `–`) separate
   words; other attached punctuation and soft hyphens stay included. Both modes preserve combining
   marks, surrogate pairs, and emoji sequences. Reuse the mode through page turns and gestures;
   later metadata refreshes take effect on the next book open, never partway through a drag.
10. Word lookup crosses inline formatting, links, highlight marks, and `<wbr>`, but stops at
    paragraph/list-item boundaries, hard line breaks, and inline images. Dragging between units
    retains the previous range; crossing the anchor reverses direction and keeps at least one
    complete unit. Grabbing a handle anchors to the complete unit at the opposite end; overlapping
    touch targets choose the nearer handle. Painted range, captured excerpt, and locator describe
    the same text; offsets remain UTF-16 characters.

## Data & sync
No persistence of its own; produces ReaderRangeLocator (spec 2.4) consumed by highlights and
ai-comments buckets. Pure client state via context.

## Edge cases
Empty/collapsed selection; selection spanning blocks or chapters; selection over an image; selection
lost on reflow/resize; touch vs mouse selection; a touch selection-drag whose horizontal travel
exceeds the swipe threshold must select, not page (mobile-only — the collision that caused
wrong-fragment / opposite-direction selections and a silently turned page behind the panel).

## Capture invariants (keep these true)
Touch capture is fragile because browsers fire a *storm* of events per gesture and differ across iOS
/ Android. Keep these to avoid the wrong-fragment / double-open / callout-over-panel regressions this
file keeps hitting (see [[glossary]] for terms):
- **One check per gesture.** Every capture path goes through the *settle scheduler*'s single slot —
  never read `window.getSelection()` directly from a handler, or the burst one gesture emits
  (`touchend` then a synthetic `mouseup`) re-opens the panel twice.
- **Gate every read on container containment:** the event target must be inside the page box.
  `touchend` reports the node the finger went down on, so this also keeps a tap on the
  panel/backdrop from being read as a selection.
- **Capture never mutates the selection — the panel owns the drop.** Every capture carries its
  `pointer` kind; the AI toolbox, being the reason the *native callout* must not render, removes
  the live selection in a pre-paint effect when it opens against a touch capture
  (`selection/use-drop-live-selection`), so callout and panel never coexist. Mouse captures keep
  their selection — desktop has no callout.
- **Never assume the platform paints the selection.** On iOS the visible tint is system chrome,
  not our `::selection` rule: forcing that rule to a solid colour, to `transparent !important`, or
  setting `user-select: none` on a live selection all leave it unchanged, and the engine's own
  paint capture shows no tint at all for selections plainly visible on screen. It is not drawn for
  a block fragment continued from a previous column, and `transform`/`scrollLeft` pagination and
  forced repaints do not change that. `CSS.highlights` paints where the system won't — but only
  while the subtree is selectable, so it is not available under `user-select: none`; positioned
  rects are (measured on device 2026-08-31).
- **Tune delays in `selection/capture/timing.ts`,** never inline — today only
  `IMMEDIATE_SETTLE_MS`.
- **Known gap:** a *compatibility (synthetic) mouse event* echoing a real touch selection can land
  in the scheduler's single slot after that gesture's touch check and re-read it with the mouse
  rules; the capture then carries `pointer: "mouse"`, so the toolbox would not drop the live
  selection and the callout could sit over the panel. Unverified on device — device traces did
  show touch-burst events landing over a second late. If it shows up, gate mouse checks on
  time-since-last-touch in timing.ts.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] On touch, the selection highlight is visible through the *entire* creation — from the initial
      word select, while sliding, and across handle adjustments — on every page, including pages
      that open mid-paragraph. On iOS this is the app's own paint (Behaviour 8); relying on the
      platform to draw it fails there and must not be reintroduced.
- [ ] The AI toolbox opens on release; the selection and its callout vanish exactly when it opens,
      never before.
- [ ] A text selection yields a correct range locator with usable context fallback.
- [ ] Both highlight and AI overlays anchor to the same selection.
- [ ] Selection clears on navigation/dismiss without leaking stale state.
- [ ] On touch, extending or adjusting a selection never turns the page; a swipe with no active
      selection still navigates.
- [ ] iOS drag and handle adjustments add/remove whole words in both directions, including crossing
      the anchor, dash-separated words, formatted words, and repeated whitespace.
- [ ] Book language determines word/character mode once, including legacy/missing metadata and
      foreign-language quotations. Combining marks and emoji are never split; Android and desktop
      retain native selection behaviour.

## Open questions
