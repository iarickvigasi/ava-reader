## Summary
Lays rendered content into discrete pages/columns and decides which page is shown, including after a
navigation or resume request.

## Scope
- In: column/page layout, geometric measurement of content, splitting a chapter into pages, choosing
  the current page, reflow on resize/font change, honouring a pending restore intent.
- Non-goals: rendering blocks (see content-rendering), choosing the target position (see
  navigation/resume).

## Behaviour
1. Content is measured (measurement/) to compute page boundaries for the current viewport and font
   scale.
2. resolvePaginationDecision picks currentPageIndex from inputs (active chapter, restore intent,
   page resolution: exact | block-start).
3. A pending RestoreIntent pins the page to a block/edge until consumed. A sticky edge intent stays
   pinned across relayout only while the reader is still on that edge; once they page away, the pin
   releases so later relayouts preserve their current position (visible locator) instead of snapping
   back to the edge (shouldKeepStickyRestorePinned).
4. Resize or font-scale change recomputes the layout key (createPaginationLayoutKey) and
   re-paginates, keeping the reader near the same block.
5. Pages move horizontally only. The reader shell is a non-scrolling viewport-height box, so the
   document is pinned against overscroll while a reader route is mounted
   (useLockDocumentOverscroll) — otherwise mobile WebKit rubber-bands the page on a slightly
   off-axis swipe, and that vertical travel changes the visual viewport, which re-keys the
   measurement cache and re-paginates mid-swipe (see Edge cases).
6. Page positioning: the current page is shown by offsetting the single multicolumn article with
   relative positioning (`left` in use-article-style.ts), a paint-only shift that leaves geometry
   unchanged (same width/column count; everything downstream keys off the discrete currentPageIndex).
   It deliberately avoids a `transform`: a translate3d GPU-composites the article, and mobile WebKit
   then fails to repaint the text-selection highlight into the layer's stale tiles mid-drag, so the
   highlight goes invisible (see 1.6-selection-bridge). A future page-turn animation re-introduces a
   transform transiently — composited only while the slide runs — see open questions.

## Data & sync
Pure functions over measurement results + RestoreIntent (from navigation.ts).
PaginationDecisionInput → PaginationDecision. No I/O.

## Edge cases
Measurement failure (status failed) → fallback to block-start; viewport mid-resize; font change
mid-read; chapter shorter than one page; restore intent referencing a missing block; a mobile
viewport change (address-bar show/hide) that re-measures mid-chapter must not snap a reader who has
paged away back to the chapter edge; a touch text-selection drag must not be read as a page swipe —
the swipe handler bails while a selection is active (see 1.6-selection-bridge).

**Known defect (open):** a swipe forward can leave the reader on an earlier page. A viewport change
re-keys the measurement cache; during the re-measure the visible locator stops being published while
page steps are still accepted, so the restore's absolute setCurrentPageIndex resolves the reader
back to the last recorded position. Overscroll locking (Behaviour §5) removes one trigger, not the
defect. The exact user-visible sequence is not yet confirmed against a device — reproduced in
Chromium only, and the reported symptom does not match that reproduction.

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Resizing or changing font re-paginates and keeps the reader on the same block.
- [ ] A restore intent lands on the correct page, then is consumed.
- [ ] Measurement failure degrades to block-start without crashing.
- [ ] Once the reader pages away from a chapter edge, a relayout/re-measure keeps them on their
      current page — it never jumps back to the first/last page.
- [ ] The visible-page offset does not GPU-composite the article at rest, so the selection highlight
      paints on every page and screen region (mobile; see 1.6-selection-bridge).
- [ ] A swipe with a vertical component moves the page horizontally or not at all — the reader never
      travels vertically on mobile.
- [ ] A forward swipe never lands the reader on an earlier page (blocked on the known defect above).

## Open questions
Scroll vs paged mode toggle; two-page spread rules on wide screens; page-turn animation via transient
compositing (add a transform + transition only while sliding, then demote); cross-page text selection
(auto-advance the page when a selection drag reaches the edge).
